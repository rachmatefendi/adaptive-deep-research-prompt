# Evidence policy and traceability

This document explains how to interpret the evidence controls in the [canonical prompt](../PROMPT.md). It is guidance for readers, not a separate source of factual evidence.

## Claim categories

| Category | Meaning | Reporting rule |
|---|---|---|
| Verified Fact | Claim directly supported by material actually examined | Cite the examined source and limit the claim to what was checked |
| Source-Derived Claim | Claim attributed to a source but not independently established | Name the source and avoid stating it as independently confirmed |
| Assumption | Working premise used in analysis | Make explicit if material to the conclusion |
| Inference | Analytical conclusion drawn from evidence | Explain reasoning and conditions |
| Estimate / Projection | Modeled result | State formulas/inputs, period, assumptions, uncertainty |
| Recommendation | Proposed action supported by findings | Identify trade-offs, authority boundaries, and validation needs |
| Evidence Gap | Missing, inaccessible, weak, or conflicting evidence | Explain how it affects confidence and what would resolve it |

## Source tiers are heuristics

Tier 1: direct original evidence, primary legal texts, official disclosures, original datasets and studies. Tier 2: independent high-quality analytical work. Tier 3: contextual secondary interpretation. Tier 4: anecdotal, promotional, exploratory, or unverified content.

A tier cannot substitute for examining *fitness for the specific claim*, methods, independence, timeliness, and conflicts of interest. One primary dataset may not be reliable or complete; a systematic review may be stronger for a synthesis question.

## Material claim sufficiency

Default verification target, where applicable: one directly examined, authoritative original source **or** two genuinely independent high-quality analytical sources. Do not treat this as a mathematical pass/fail rule. A source's relevance, study design, and the consequence of error matter more than meeting a count. If the evidence is insufficient, qualify the claim rather than inventing corroboration.

## Circular citations: simple example

- Website A repeats a regulator's press release.
- Report B summarizes Website A.
- Commentary C quotes Report B.

A, B, and C are *not three independent confirmations*. Trace the chain to the press release and check the primary regulator record, date, and exact scope. A press release may not be equivalent to the controlling rule or effective regulation.

## Access and verification states

- **ACCESSIBLE / VERIFIED:** original or relevant content examined.
- **PARTIALLY ACCESSED:** preview, excerpt, or abstract only.
- **IDENTIFIED / NOT ACCESSED:** source located but original content not obtained.
- **RESTRICTED:** paywall, subscription, permission, authentication, or other access block.
- **NOT FOUND:** search did not locate the identified source; this does not prove nonexistence.
- **UNVERIFIED:** authenticity, existence, or material content not established.

If a primary source is restricted, cite any secondary reporting *as secondary reporting*, not as verified primary content.

## Traceable citations

Prefer inline source links immediately after material factual claims. Where available, capture issuing body, title, publication/effective dates, URL or document ID, and a verified section/page. Never invent a page, DOI, regulation number, version, or link.

Keep a Source Register for material sources. Distinguish citation quantity from independence and quality.

## Evidence integrity under context pressure

Preserve the primary research question, source register, access status, claim classification, counter-evidence, and unresolved contradictions before shortening prose, background, or formatting.
